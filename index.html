About

Who I Am

I'm an Sc.M. Biostatistics student at the Brown University School of Public Health and a Mathematics-Statistics graduate of Whitman College. Before Brown, I taught Logic & Computation to high school seniors at Great Hearts Trivium Prep and worked as a Symbolic AI Research Trainee at the Naval Postgraduate School through the Oak Ridge Institute for Science and Education, where I integrated large language models with formal logic systems for military stakeholders.

I grew up in Chimanimani, in rural Zambia, where I earned the nickname "Chisuntamatics" for my curiosity about mathematics. Technology was expensive and education about it was scarce, so the link between math and the inequities I saw every day felt distant. In 2016, the Yale Young African Scholars program in Zimbabwe showed me how much access to tools and knowledge shapes what students can become, and it set me on the path to studying in the United States.

Originally from Zambia, I bring a cross-cultural perspective to everything I do. Growing up with frequent power outages caused by climate-driven disruptions to hydroelectric infrastructure gave me a personal understanding of the urgency around energy security and sustainability. That experience, combined with co-founding a climate-focused NGO, drives my interest in using data to advance equitable, sustainable solutions.

My work spans sports analytics, economic modeling, computational biology, symbolic AI, and education. I thrive in interdisciplinary environments where I can adapt quickly to new domains and translate complex quantitative insights into actionable decisions for diverse audiences.

Research Interests

The Full Life Cycle of Data

I believe the success of a model begins at the very inception of data collection. My interests follow data from the moment it is gathered to the decisions built on it, especially in places where resources are scarce.

Constraint-First Statistical Methods

Designing methods that treat data sparsity, collection limits, and structural constraints as design parameters rather than violations of assumptions.

Data Quality and Official Statistics

Survey design, data validation, and statistical infrastructure for national statistics systems. At the Zambia Statistical Agency, I traced anomalies in national price data back to gaps in field infrastructure and training, and that experience shapes how I think about where data comes from.

Interpretable and Equitable Modeling

Machine learning that stakeholders can understand and question, built to reflect the complexity of the communities it serves.

Causal Inference and Bayesian Methods

Drawing reliable conclusions from observational, confounded, and sparse data, and making honest use of prior knowledge when data are limited.

Researching Zambia for Zambia: I want my work to return practical benefit to the communities it studies, not simply describe them.
